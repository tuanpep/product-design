# Evidence and decision contract

## Classify claims

- **Given:** a claim explicitly supplied by the human. Attribute it; a stakeholder's statement is not automatically an observed user need.
- **Observed:** supported by a named source, actual research, or a test. Record where, when, and what was observed. A web page about a similar product is not proof that these users behave the same way.
- **Inferred:** reasoned from stated evidence; show the inference and its uncertainty.
- **Proposed:** an agent recommendation, including product rules, copy, thresholds, and interactions not yet approved.
- **Unknown:** needed information not available; state why it matters and how to learn it.

Separate **design coherence** (the proposed pieces agree), **evidence confidence** (the important claims have credible support), and **delivery readiness** (the next slice is feasible and sufficiently specified). Passing one is not passing the others.

## Decision authority

The agent may recommend a problem framing, options, provisional design, and a first release. It cannot manufacture user research, cost figures, legal interpretation, feasibility approval, business-rule approval, or the decision to invest. Identify the human owner of consequential choices. Mark an unavailable owner as unknown rather than impersonating one. [Advise mode](advise-mode.md) may recommend a full provisional solution and design without waiting. It still cannot approve that recommendation.

Keep sources connected to specific claims, not only in a bibliography. Do not turn a plan to test into a test result. If available evidence is weak, finish an honest proposal with a test plan rather than declaring the product validated.

## Research grounding

Use primary sources for standards and policy claims where possible. Relevant foundations include the [Design Council Double Diamond](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/the-double-diamond/) for iterative exploration and convergence, [ISO 9241-210:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/77520.html) for human-centred design over the lifecycle, [ISO 9241-11:2018](https://www.iso.org/standard/63500.html) for usability as an outcome of use, [ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018](https://www.iso.org/standard/72089.html) for the shape of a requirement, [GOV.UK discovery](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-discovery-phase-works) and [alpha](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-alpha-phase-works) for learning before building, [GOV.UK user stories](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/writing-user-stories) for build slices, and [W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) for web accessibility. These guide the method; they do not validate a particular product idea. The working sequence is in [workflow.md](workflow.md). When options conflict, [principles.md](principles.md) decides which outcome wins.
