### Revise an existing design

**You own the update. Withdraw a stale readiness claim before you edit.**

1. Read the existing proposal, including its decision, evidence, unresolved risks, and status. Read the newly supplied information verbatim.
2. Name the earliest premise that changed. List the dependent claims, options, journey steps, rules, measures, first-release scope, and checks affected by it.
3. Revisit only the affected work. `frame-problem` when the problem itself changed. `discover-needs` for new context. `define-requirements` for needs or requirements. `explore-solutions` when the chosen approach is open again. `design-experience` for the experience. `drawio` when a journey flow or screen prototype changed. `plan-evaluation` when the risky assumption or its test changed. `specify-build` when the slice or its checks changed. Read the principle leaf whose trigger the new evidence hits. Preserve unaffected evidence and decisions. Do not silently promote a proposed choice to an approved one.
4. Update the proposal in place. Mark superseded claims and the reason for the new recommendation in its decision history. Reconcile optional prototypes with the text. Do not overwrite human edits without agreement.
5. `review-design` and `decide-next` on the whole result.

**Reply:** what changed, which principle changed a choice, what remains unverified, and who makes the next decision.
