### New idea

**You own the proposal, not the decision to build.**

1. `frame-problem`. Keep the proposed feature distinct from the need. Read **principle-start-from-the-outcome**.
2. `discover-needs`. Inspect supplied material and the target project's relevant context. Research externally only for facts that could change the recommendation. Record source links and dates.
3. `define-requirements`. Leave needs stable and broader than features.
4. `decide-next`. If the recommendation is stop or repeat discovery, write that proposal and do not invent a product.
5. `explore-solutions`. Include the current approach or no-build baseline and at least one credible alternative when there is a real choice. Read **principle-see-the-whole-service**.
6. `design-experience` for the recommended approach. Read **principle-behave-predictably**. Where a journey or screen sequence is materially uncertain, `drawio` for the flow chart or prototype, just enough to expose the assumption.
7. `plan-evaluation` for the assumption that would reverse the recommendation. Record the plan as a plan.
8. `specify-build` only when specifying a production slice is the defensible next step. Otherwise retain evaluation depth: disposable learning work and deferred production scope. Planning a test does not satisfy it. Define the first useful outcome, what waits, and how to check the slice without promoting untested assumptions to facts.
9. Write `product-design.md` using [the proposal format](../references/proposal-format.md). Then `review-design` and `decide-next`. Fix internal contradictions before reporting.

A small idea can satisfy a skill in a few sentences. A skipped step stays visible with the reason. Do not block a useful proposal on facts the agent cannot obtain. If two goals conflict and a reversible default cannot be defended, ask one focused question. Otherwise recommend a labeled provisional choice and continue.

**Reply:** the recommendation, the principle that decided the trade-off and the choice it changed, the strongest evidence, the weakest assumption, and the next human decision.
